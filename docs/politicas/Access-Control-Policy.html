1. Purpose

This policy defines who may access which systems and data, and how access is granted, reviewed and removed. Its central rule is the principle of least privilege: every person and system receives only the access required for its legitimate task, for as long as it is required. It applies in particular to personal data.

2. Principles

  • Need to know: access to personal data is granted only to roles whose duties require it.
  • Least privilege: permissions are the minimum needed, and administrative rights are exceptional.
  • Individual accountability: every account belongs to one person or one service; shared accounts are not used for systems that hold customer data.
  • Separation of tenants: a customer company can never see another company's data.
  • Traceability: access to and changes of sensitive data are recorded.

3. Access to the platform by customer users

The platform uses role-based access control. The server checks the user's permissions on every request; the screen only hides what the user cannot do. There is no public sign-up: accounts are created by the customer company's administrator.

  • Personal data of end customers (names, documents, contacts, addresses) is available only to roles that hold the customer permissions; roles without them cannot read it.
  • Customer administrators create users, assign roles and remove access. The platform records these changes in the audit trail.
  • Sign-in uses a password that follows the platform's password rules, account lockout after repeated failures, and optional two-factor authentication.
  • Data of different customer companies is isolated at the database level (row-level security).

5. Granting, changing and removing access

  • Granting: access is requested for a specific role and duty, approved by the Information Security Officer (staff) or by the customer's administrator (customer users), and set up with the minimum permissions needed.
  • Changing: when duties change, permissions are adjusted the same day and the previous ones removed.
  • Removal: when a person leaves the company or a customer user is no longer authorized, access is disabled the same day.
  • Temporary access: elevated access for a specific task is time-limited and removed when the task ends.

6. Review and monitoring

  • The Information Security Officer reviews all staff access to production systems at least quarterly and removes what is no longer needed.
  • Customers are encouraged to review their own users and roles periodically; the platform lists users and roles for this purpose.
  • Sign-ins, permission and role changes, integration connections and other sensitive actions are recorded in the audit trail, with user, time and company. Unusual access is investigated under the Incident Response Policy.

7. Exceptions and enforcement

Exceptions must be requested in writing, assessed for risk, approved by management and have an end date. Access that violates this policy is removed immediately, and violations may lead to disciplinary action or contract termination.
